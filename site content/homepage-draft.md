## Why Houston Attics Are Hard on Insulation

Houston attics work harder than most. Summers are long and humid, many homes are older, and storms can knock out power for days. Those facts decide how much insulation your attic needs.

### A cooling climate, not a heating one

We use Bush Intercontinental Airport (IAH) on the north side for weather numbers, because it sits closer to Cypress than Hobby Airport does. NOAA's [1991 to 2020 climate normals](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/land-based-station/us-climate-normals) for IAH show about 111 days a year at or above 90°F and about 7 days at or above 100°F. The average July high is 94.5°F. The average July low is still 75.7°F.

Houston needs about 3,300 cooling degree days a year. A cooling degree day is a measure of how much A/C a place needs. Heating needs only about 1,270. Recent years ran hotter than normal. IAH reached 100°F on 45 days in 2023, and it logged 145 days at or above 90°F in 2025, based on NOAA daily records.

Harris, Fort Bend and Montgomery counties all sit in Climate Zone 2A, which means hot and humid, according to the [U.S. Department of Energy](https://www.energy.gov/sites/prod/files/2015/10/f27/ba_climate_region_guide_7.3.pdf). Humid air and roof leaks are a bad mix for an attic. Insulation that has been soaked usually has to come out.

### Older homes, newer subdivisions

The median home in the City of Houston was built in 1982, according to the [U.S. Census Bureau](https://data.census.gov/table/ACSDT5Y2024.B25035?g=160XX00US4835000) (2020 to 2024 estimates). The [City of Houston Planning Department's tally](https://www.houstontx.gov/planning/Demographics/2024-council-district-tables/27-year-structure-built.pdf) of the same data shows about 47 percent of homes were built before 1980. Humble's median home also dates to 1982, and the [Harris County median is 1988](https://data.census.gov/table/ACSDT5Y2024.B25035?g=050XX00US48201).

Older attics have had decades of roof repairs, leaks and animal visits. That is why we measure and look before we suggest anything.

Newer areas near US 290 tell a different story. In the ZIP code 77429 area of Cypress, the median home was built in 2003. There, the question is whether the depth you have still matches current [ENERGY STAR advice](https://www.energystar.gov/saveathome/seal_insulate/identify-problems-you-want-fix/diy-checks-inspections/insulation-r-values): R-49 for an uninsulated attic, or R-38 if you already have 3 to 4 inches.

### Storms and long outages

Hurricane Beryl came ashore near Matagorda on July 8, 2024, then crossed the western side of the Houston area, according to the [National Hurricane Center](https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/data/tcr/AL022024_Beryl.pdf). About 2.2 million CenterPoint customers lost power. The [Public Utility Commission of Texas](https://interchange.puc.texas.gov/Documents/56822_198_1444252.PDF) reports that outages fell below 10,000 customers on July 19, 11 days after landfall. A derecho on May 16, 2024 had already cut power to about 920,000 CenterPoint customers, and the same report says restoration was essentially complete by May 23.

Insulation does not replace air conditioning. It slows how fast roof heat reaches your rooms, so your house warms up more slowly when the power is out.

Rain matters too. On April 17 and 18, 2016, the [National Weather Service](https://www.weather.gov/media/hgx/stormdata/apr16hgx.pdf) recorded 10 to 20 inches of rain in about 12 hours across northwest Harris County, where Cypress sits. A leaking roof after a storm like that is one reason old insulation has to come out.
