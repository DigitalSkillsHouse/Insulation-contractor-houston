## Home Insulation Services in Houston, TX

As a Houston insulation contractor, we cover attics, walls, and commercial buildings. Every service starts with a free walkthrough and a written quote.

### Blown in insulation
Loose fiberglass blown across the attic floor to an even depth, so it fills around pipes, wires, and framing.
- **Best for:** attics with thin or settled insulation and an open floor
- **What you get:** the depth in inches, the R-value you reach, and the price in writing
- **Good to know:** blown in fiberglass does not stop air leaks, so we seal gaps first

### Spray foam insulation
Open-cell and closed-cell foam for attics, walls, homes, and commercial buildings. Foam seals air leaks as it goes on.
- **Best for:** roof decks, walls, and homes with air leaks
- **What you get:** insulation and air sealing in one step
- **Good to know:** closed-cell resists moisture, and open-cell costs less

### Attic insulation removal & replacement
We remove old attic insulation, and we also remove spray foam when it has failed or been installed poorly. Attic insulation replacement includes both the removal of old, degraded insulation and the installation of new blown-in material.
- **Best for:** wet, dirty, damaged, or worn out insulation
- **What you get:** a clean attic floor and the old material hauled away, plus new blown-in insulation when you choose replacement
- **Good to know:** if the old insulation is dry and in good shape, we may add on top, so removal is not always needed

### Injection foam insulation
Foam injected through small holes into closed wall cavities, so we do not open the walls.
- **Best for:** finished walls with little or no insulation
- **What you get:** insulation in the walls without tearing out drywall
- **Good to know:** spray foam wall insulation goes into open framing instead

### Air sealing
We seal gaps around can lights, wiring, plumbing, and the attic hatch so conditioned air stays inside.
- **Best for:** homes with drafts, dusty attic hatches, or uneven room temperatures
- **What you get:** leaks sealed before new insulation goes down
- **Good to know:** insulation slows heat, but only sealing stops air

### Attic fan & ventilation
Solar attic fans and attic ventilation fans that move hot air out of the attic.
- **Best for:** hot attics with weak airflow
- **What you get:** a check of your soffit vents and attic airflow before we choose a fan
- **Good to know:** a fan moves heat out, but it does not replace insulation

### Commercial building insulation
Insulation for shops, offices, and other commercial buildings.
- **Best for:** commercial owners and managers who need a written price for a building
- **What you get:** a quote after we see the space
- **Good to know:** we measure the building before we recommend a material


## Is Spray Foam or Blown-In Better for a Houston Attic?

Blown-in fiberglass costs less and works well on the attic floor. Spray foam costs more, seals air leaks, and can go on the roof deck. Pick based on your budget, whether your ducts run through the attic, and how long you plan to stay. We price both for your attic so you can compare.

| | Blown-in fiberglass | Open-cell spray foam | Closed-cell spray foam |
|---|---|---|---|
| Upfront cost | Lowest | Middle | Highest |
| Seals air leaks | No, needs air sealing first | Yes | Yes |
| Moisture | Not a barrier | Not a barrier | Resists moisture |
| Where it goes | Attic floor | Roof deck or walls | Roof deck or walls |
| R-value per inch | About 2.2 to 2.9 | About 3.5 to 3.9 | About 6 to 7 |

R-value measures how well insulation resists heat flow. Higher is better. The bag label on the insulation we install lists the exact R-value per inch.

| Your attic now | Target | Total blown-in depth | Depth to add |
|---|---|---|---|
| Little or no insulation | R-49 | About 17 to 22 inches | About 17 to 22 inches |
| 3 to 4 inches in place | R-38 | About 13 to 17 inches | About 9 to 14 inches |

New homes inside the City of Houston must meet R-49 at the ceiling under the 2021 energy code, in effect since January 1, 2024.

Total depth comes from dividing the target R-value by 2.2 to 2.9 per inch. Depth to add assumes the insulation already in your attic has a similar R-value per inch.

## Why Houston Homes Need Better Attic Insulation

Houston homes need better attic insulation because the roof bakes in the sun for months, and thin insulation lets that heat into your rooms. Houston averages about 111 days a year at 90°F or higher. More insulation keeps upstairs rooms cooler, and with air sealing it cuts heating and cooling costs by about 9%.

What that means for your home:

- **A long cooling season.** Your AC fights hot weather from late spring into fall, so a weak attic costs you for many months.
- **Old insulation wears out.** Over the years it flattens, shifts, or gets wet, and a thin layer slows far less heat than it did when it was new.
- **Ducts in the attic.** If your AC ducts run through the attic, they sit in the hottest air in the house, and thicker insulation helps protect them.

## Where Do Houston Homes Leak Air?

Air leaks let cool air out and hot attic air in. Insulation slows heat, but it does not stop air, so we seal first and insulate second. Air sealing and insulation work best together.

The usual leak spots in a Houston attic are:

- Recessed can lights
- Gaps around wires and plumbing pipes
- The attic hatch or pull down stairs
- Duct boots and bath fan housings
- The tops of interior walls

We seal these with foam or caulk before any blown-in fiberglass goes down. Insulation that sits on top of open gaps loses much of its value.

## Attic Insulation Questions Houston Homeowners Ask

<details open>
<summary>Is it cheaper to blow in attic insulation myself?</summary>

Yes, it costs less upfront because you skip the labor, but you still pay for materials and a machine rental. The savings shrink if you miss air leaks or skip baffles, because insulation over open gaps loses much of its value. A pro covers those steps as part of the plan.

Doing it yourself means finding and sealing leaks first and installing baffles at the eaves so the vents stay clear. Attics can pass 130°F in summer, and the job takes a full day or more. A crew usually finishes faster and does the work in that heat for you.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Do I need to leave the house during insulation installation?</summary>

You can stay home during blown-in fiberglass installation, but leaving during the work is better. For spray foam, leave during the work and stay out until the product's re-entry time passes. We give you that time in writing before we start.

Safety agencies advise leaving during spray foam installation and staying out for at least 24 hours. Re-entry times depend on the product. [CONFIRM WITH YOUR FOAM DATA SHEET]
</details>

<details>
<summary>Why do Houston attics get so hot?</summary>

Houston attics get hot because the roof soaks up the sun, and a sun-baked roof surface can pass 150°F. Thin insulation lets that heat move down into your rooms, so your AC runs longer to fight it. Houston also averages about 7 days a year at 100°F or higher.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Does attic insulation help with humidity in Houston?</summary>

Not by itself. Insulation slows heat, but it does not remove moisture. Houston gets about 52 inches of rain a year. Sealing air leaks, keeping vents clear, and fixing roof leaks do more for moisture. We check all three on the walkthrough.
</details>

<summary>What R-value does a Houston attic need?</summary>

A Houston attic needs R-49 if it has little or no insulation, and R-38 if it already has 3 to 4 inches. ENERGY STAR recommends both numbers for our climate zone. In blown-in fiberglass, that means about 17 to 22 inches total for R-49, or about 9 to 14 more inches for R-38.
