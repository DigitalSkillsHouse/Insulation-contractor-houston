Why Blown-In Fiberglass Is the Right Choice for Most Cypress Attics

Cypress sits in IECC Climate Zone 2A — one of the hottest and most humid climates in the continental United States. Attic temperatures in Cypress regularly reach 135–142°F on summer afternoons. At those temperatures, the difference between R-13 and R-38 insulation determines whether your AC can keep up.

1
No gaps around obstacles. Cypress attics are full of wiring, plumbing stacks, cross-bracing, and HVAC ducts. Blown-in fiberglass fills every cavity and contours to every irregular surface. Batt insulation has to be cut around these obstacles — it almost always leaves gaps, and gaps in Cypress's summer heat are direct energy-loss paths into your living space.
2
Can be added directly over existing insulation. Most Cypress homes have some insulation already. As long as the existing material is dry and clean, blown-in fiberglass goes right on top — no tearout, no dumpster, no extra day of work. We measure the current depth, calculate what's needed to reach R-38 or R-49, and blow in the difference.
3
40–60% less expensive than spray foam. Open-cell spray foam costs $1.20–$1.85/sq ft and closed-cell runs $2.20–$3.10. Blown-in fiberglass at $0.65–$0.95/sq ft delivers the same R-value improvement for the attic floor at a fraction of the cost. Spray foam is better when HVAC equipment is in the attic — for everything else, blown-in wins on value.
