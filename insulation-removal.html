When Does Attic Insulation Need to Be Removed?

Not every old attic needs a full tear-out. But in these situations, removal is the only right answer:

  • Pest contamination — rodent droppings, urine, and nesting material saturate blown-in insulation. Topping off traps the contamination.
  • Moisture or mold — wet insulation loses R-value and harbors mold. It must come out before the source is repaired.
  • Fire or smoke damage — smoke odors are absorbed by insulation fibers and won't go away until the material is removed.
  • Vermiculite (pre-1985) — older vermiculite insulation may contain asbestos. Remediation requires full removal by certified contractors.
  • Extreme compression — insulation so flat and compacted that its current R-value is below R-11 and air-sealing cannot be done through it.

In all other cases, where existing insulation is clean and undamaged, we can top off with blown-in to reach R-38 or R-49 — no removal needed.

Our Removal Process

  1. Free inspection — we assess the attic, measure depth, identify contamination or damage, and confirm whether removal is necessary.
  2. Protection — floors and living areas are protected; HVAC returns are sealed to prevent fiber migration into the home.
  3. Vacuum removal — commercial insulation vacuum removes all blown-in material. Batts are manually pulled and bagged.
  4. Surface clean — attic floor swept and inspected for remaining debris.
  5. Air sealing — all penetrations, gaps at top plates, and bypasses are sealed with foam and mastic before new insulation is installed.
  6. Reinstallation — new blown-in fiberglass or open-cell spray foam installed to R-38 or R-49 as specified.
