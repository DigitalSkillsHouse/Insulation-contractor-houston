Blown-In vs Fiberglass vs Spray Foam: Which Attic Insulation Is Best for Cypress TX Homes?

Published October 14, 2026  ·  By Insulation Contractor Houston

For most Cypress TX attics, blown-in insulation is the best choice — it fills every joist bay and gap, reaches R-49 in one visit, and costs less per R-value gained than any alternative. Spray foam is the right call when your HVAC equipment lives inside the attic. Fiberglass batts work for new construction but underperform in existing attics with irregular framing. This guide shows you exactly which type fits your situation.

Why the Choice Matters More in Cypress Than in Cooler Climates

Cypress sits in IECC Climate Zone 2A — hot and humid. Your attic air temperature on a July afternoon can reach 140°F to 160°F. Every type of insulation resists that heat differently, and the gaps between materials show up on your electric bill from June through September, not just in a specification sheet.

The DOE recommends R-38 to R-49 for attics in this climate zone. Most Cypress homes built before 2000 have R-11 to R-22 — well below that range regardless of what type of insulation is already there. The question is not just "which type is best in general" but "which type gets this specific attic to R-49 at the lowest installed cost with the least disruption."

Blown-In Insulation: Best for Most Cypress TX Attic Upgrades

Blown-in insulation — fiberglass or cellulose — is the most common attic upgrade we install in the Cy-Fair area, and for good reason. A machine blows loose material across the attic floor, filling every joist bay, working around cross-bracing, covering pipes and wires, and reaching into corners that batts cannot fit into.

Why it outperforms batts in existing attics

Fiberglass batts are designed for uniform stud cavities in new construction. An existing Cypress attic built in the 1980s or 1990s has irregular joist spacing, blocked runs, HVAC penetrations, and ceiling fans — none of which batts cover cleanly. Blown-in fills around everything. The result is a higher whole-assembly R-value, not just a higher label R-value.

Blown-in over existing insulation

If your attic has existing fiberglass batts that are dry and undamaged, blown-in can go directly on top to bring the total depth up to R-49. We check the existing material during the free walkthrough — if it is wet, compressed, or pest-contaminated, we remove it first. Adding new material over a damaged base does not fix the underlying problem.

Blown-in fiberglass vs blown-in cellulose

Both are good options. Blown-in fiberglass (what we primarily install) does not retain moisture, is non-combustible, and does not settle as much as cellulose over time. Cellulose is made from recycled paper, carries a higher R-value per inch, and is denser, which makes it slightly better at reducing air movement through the material. In Cypress TX's humid climate, fiberglass's lower moisture retention gives it a small edge for long-term performance.

Blown-in is the right choice when: your attic is an existing vented attic, your HVAC equipment is in the house (not the attic), your existing insulation is dry and at least partially intact, and you want the lowest cost-per-R-value installed. Learn more on our blown-in insulation page.

Fiberglass Batts: When They Work and When They Don't

Fiberglass batts — the pink or yellow rectangular pieces most people picture when they think of attic insulation — are the original standard for residential insulation. They are pre-cut, easy to handle, and work well in one specific situation: a new attic with uniform framing and nothing installed yet.

The problem with batts in existing Cypress attics

Batts are rigid. They do not conform to irregular shapes. When a batt meets a cross-brace, an HVAC penetration, or a joist bay that is slightly narrower than standard, gaps appear. In Cypress TX, where attic air reaches 150°F in summer, those gaps are pathways for heat to bypass the insulation entirely.

There is also a compression problem. A batt pressed against an obstruction loses R-value where it is compressed. Older batts that have settled, gotten damp once, or been walked on repeatedly perform far below their label rating.

Where batts still make sense

New construction, where studs and joists are freshly framed and spacing is uniform, is the right use case for batts. A new Cypress home being framed today will have batt insulation installed in wall cavities and sometimes in attic knee walls — both situations where the framing is regular and the material is installed before anything else goes in. For an existing attic that needs an upgrade, blown-in almost always makes more sense.

Spray Foam Insulation: The Right Call for Specific Situations

Spray foam costs two to three times as much as blown-in per square foot of attic. That premium is worth paying in specific situations — and usually not worth paying otherwise.

When spray foam is clearly the better choice

HVAC equipment or ductwork in the attic. This is the most common reason we recommend spray foam in Cypress TX homes. If your air handler or ductwork runs through the attic, that equipment is sitting in 150-degree air in July. Open-cell spray foam applied to the underside of the roof deck creates a sealed, conditioned attic — ductwork and equipment are now inside the thermal envelope, not outside it. The efficiency gain on the HVAC system alone often justifies the cost within a few years.

Roof deck air sealing. Spray foam on the roof deck seals every gap, crack, and penetration at the same time it insulates. For homes with heavy air leakage that a standard air sealing job cannot fully address, roof deck encapsulation eliminates the problem from above.

Post-hurricane structural upgrade. Closed-cell spray foam adds rigidity to the roof deck and is sometimes installed after storm damage as part of a resilience upgrade. It also acts as a Class II vapor retarder — important in certain roofing configurations.

Open-cell vs closed-cell in a Cypress attic

Open-cell spray foam (R-3.7 per inch) is the standard choice for roof deck encapsulation — it is lighter, less expensive, and expands to fill cavities well. Closed-cell spray foam (R-6.5 per inch) achieves higher R-values in less depth and provides a vapor barrier — useful for crawl spaces, exterior walls, and situations where moisture management is the primary concern. For most Cypress attic encapsulations, open-cell is the right product. See our spray foam insulation page for full details.

Spray foam is the right choice when: your HVAC equipment or ductwork runs through the attic, you have a cathedral ceiling with no attic access, you need both air sealing and insulation in one pass, or you are doing a post-storm roof deck upgrade. For a standard vented attic without ductwork inside it, blown-in delivers the same performance at a lower cost.

Which Type Is Right for Your Cypress TX Home?

Three questions narrow it down for almost every situation:

1. Is your HVAC equipment or ductwork in the attic?

Yes → Spray foam encapsulation on the roof deck. The HVAC efficiency gain justifies the premium.
No → Continue to question 2.

2. Is the existing insulation in good condition (dry, no pests, no mold)?

Yes → Blown-in on top of the existing material to reach R-49. No removal needed.
No → Remove the damaged material first, then blown-in. Or call us — we assess during the free walkthrough.

3. Is this new construction or an existing home?

New construction → Fiberglass batts in wall cavities are standard; blown-in for the attic floor.
Existing home → Blown-in almost always. Batts cannot retrofit cleanly into an occupied attic.

If you are still unsure after those three questions, the free inspection settles it. We measure your current depth, check the condition of existing material, look for air leaks, and give you a written quote for the right solution — before any work is scheduled.

One more thing: is your upstairs noticeably hotter than downstairs?

Insulation slows heat transfer through solid surfaces — it doesn't remove the 135–140°F air already sitting in your attic. If your second floor runs 8–12°F hotter than your first floor in summer, you likely need both better insulation and mechanical attic ventilation. Our attic fan installation page explains how solar and electric fans pair with a blown-in upgrade — and why many Cypress homeowners do both in a single visit.

Why Air Sealing Matters Regardless of Which Type You Choose

Insulation slows heat transfer through solid materials. It does not stop air from moving through gaps. A Cypress attic with R-38 of blown-in but open top plates, unblocked recessed lights, and an unsealed attic hatch is still letting 150-degree air push down into your living space — the insulation is bypassed.

The DOE describes air sealing and insulation as a system, not separate jobs, because they are. For blown-in installations, we seal key penetrations before blowing the material. For spray foam on the roof deck, sealing is built into the application. Either way, skipping the air sealing step leaves a significant portion of the insulation's value unrealized.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best type of attic insulation for Cypress TX? +

For most Cypress TX homes, blown-in fiberglass or blown-in cellulose is the best attic floor insulation — cost-effective, fast to install, and capable of reaching R-49 in a single visit. Spray foam makes more sense when HVAC equipment or ductwork sits inside the attic, or when you need to encapsulate the roof deck to bring ductwork into the conditioned envelope.

Is fiberglass or blown-in insulation better for a Cypress attic? +

Blown-in outperforms fiberglass batts in most existing Cypress attics. Blown-in fills irregular joist bays and gaps that batts miss, settles less over time when installed at the correct density, and achieves higher effective R-values because it covers more of the attic surface. Fiberglass batts leave air gaps at edges and penetrations that reduce their whole-assembly performance.

How much does attic insulation cost in Cypress TX? +

Blown-in insulation to R-49 on a typical 1,800 sq ft Cypress attic runs $1,800 to $3,500 installed. Spray foam encapsulation on the same attic costs $3,500 to $7,000+ depending on roof area and foam type. These are ranges — your written quote will itemize the exact cost for your attic's size, current depth, and any removal needed.

Can I put blown-in insulation over existing fiberglass batts? +

Yes, if the existing batts are dry, undamaged, and not contaminated by pests or mold. Blown-in fiberglass can be installed directly on top of existing batts to bring the total R-value up to R-49. If the batts are wet, compressed, or pest-damaged, removal is recommended first — adding new material over a damaged base does not fix the underlying problem.

Does spray foam insulation work in Cypress TX attics? +

Yes, and it is the best choice in certain situations. Open-cell spray foam applied to the underside of the roof deck creates a sealed, conditioned attic that protects ductwork and HVAC equipment from 140-degree summer heat. For a standard vented attic without ductwork inside, blown-in is almost always more cost-effective.
