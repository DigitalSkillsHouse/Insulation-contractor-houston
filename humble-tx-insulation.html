Why Humble, TX Attics Lose So Much Energy

Humble sits in IECC Climate Zone 2A, where the DOE recommends R-38 to R-60 for attic insulation. On our July walkthroughs, attics here regularly read 135–140°F at the roof deck. Heat moves from hot surfaces to cooler ones, so an under-insulated ceiling pushes that heat into your living space all day. Harris County's humidity makes it worse: moisture moving through gaps in old batt insulation speeds up that heat transfer.

Humble has grown in waves for over a century, so homes from the 1940s, 1960s, 1980s, and 2000s sit in the same neighborhoods, each built to the insulation standard of its era. A 1978 build near Will Clayton Parkway likely still has its original R-11 fiberglass; a 2003 build in Atascocita might have R-19. Both fall well short of today's recommended R-38.

Q: What R-value is recommended for attics in Humble, TX?

The DOE recommends R-38 to R-60 for IECC Climate Zone 2A, which covers Humble and Harris County. Most Humble homes built before 2005 measure R-11 to R-19 today. R-49 is worth considering for homes with larger, west-facing roof sections, where summer heat load is highest.

Attic Insulation Services in Humble, TX

We offer four services in Humble. The right combination depends on your attic layout, your current R-value, whether your HVAC equipment sits in the attic, and what the existing insulation looks like. We check all of that before recommending anything.

Blown-In Fiberglass Insulation The right choice for most Humble homeowners upgrading an attic floor. It fills every gap around pipes, wiring, and irregular framing that batt insulation cannot reach. In Humble attics that hit 135–140°F in July, any uncovered gap is a direct heat path into your living space. A typical 1,600 to 2,200 sq ft attic floor takes 3 to 5 hours from setup to cleanup. Air Sealing Done before new insulation goes in. It closes the gaps at top plates, around plumbing and electrical penetrations, and at any point where cooled air escapes into the attic. In many older Humble homes, these gaps are the reason the attic loses its temperature separation even when insulation is present. Blown-in fiberglass on top of unsealed penetrations still underperforms.
Spray Foam Insulation The right call when your HVAC equipment or ductwork sits inside the attic. Instead of insulating the floor, spray foam goes under the roof deck and brings the attic into the insulated part of your home. Open-cell spray foam handles Humble's humidity well and costs less than closed-cell. Closed-cell is the better option when moisture resistance or structural reinforcement is the priority. Attic Insulation Removal in Humble, TX

Most Humble attics do not need removal. If existing insulation is dry, clean, and free of pest activity, new blown-in fiberglass goes directly on top. Removal is necessary when the material is wet from a roof leak, contaminated by rodents, or damaged by mold — conditions we see regularly in Humble's older homes.

Q: Do I really need to remove old insulation before adding new insulation?

Most of the time, no. We check your attic during the free walkthrough and tell you straight: if it's dry and clean, we add new material on top; if it's wet, moldy, or pest-contaminated, it comes out first. We don't recommend removal unless it's genuinely needed.

Attic Insulation Cost in Humble, TX

Blown-in fiberglass attic insulation in Humble runs $1.00 to $2.50 per square foot installed. Spray foam costs more, from $2.50 to $5.00 per square foot depending on whether it's open-cell or closed-cell. Removal, when needed, adds $1.00 to $1.50 per square foot.
