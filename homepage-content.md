## Why Houston Homes Need Better Attic Insulation

Houston homes need better attic insulation because the roof bakes in the sun for months, and thin insulation lets that heat into your rooms. Houston averages about 106.5 days a year at 90°F or higher. More insulation keeps upstairs rooms cooler, and with air sealing it cuts heating and cooling costs by about 15%.

### Does attic insulation help with humidity in Houston?

Not by itself. Insulation slows heat, but it does not remove moisture. Houston gets about 52 inches of rain a year. Sealing air leaks, keeping vents clear, and fixing roof leaks do more for moisture. We check all three on the walkthrough.

### What That Means for Your Home

- A long cooling season. Your AC fights hot weather from late spring into fall, so a weak attic costs you for many months.
- Old insulation wears out. Over the years it flattens, shifts, or gets wet, and a thin layer slows far less heat than it did when it was new.
- Ducts in the attic. If your AC ducts run through the attic, they sit in the hottest air in the house, and thicker insulation helps protect them.
### Why Older Homes Around 77080 Need a Closer Look

Many homes in Spring Branch were built in the 1950s and 1960s, and many of the western neighborhoods are brick ranch style houses. Insulation put in decades ago may be thin, flattened, or moved around by past repairs. The only way to know is to measure it.

### Signs Your Attic Needs More Insulation

- Upstairs rooms feel hotter than downstairs rooms
- The AC runs long after the sun goes down
- Electric bills climb with no other cause
- You can see the tops of the ceiling joists
- The insulation looks flat, wet, dirty, or uneven
- Dust or a musty smell comes through the attic hatch

## Is Spray Foam or Blown-In Better for a Houston Attic?

Blown-in fiberglass costs less and works well on the attic floor. Spray foam costs more, seals air leaks, and can go on the roof deck. Pick based on your budget, whether your ducts run through the attic, and how long you plan to stay. We price both for your attic so you can compare.

- Upfront cost: Blown-in fiberglass Lowest | Open-cell spray foam Middle | Closed-cell spray foam Highest
- Seals air leaks: Blown-in fiberglass No, needs air sealing first | Open-cell spray foam Yes | Closed-cell spray foam Yes
- Moisture: Blown-in fiberglass Not a barrier | Open-cell spray foam Not a barrier | Closed-cell spray foam Resists moisture
- Where it goes: Blown-in fiberglass Attic floor | Open-cell spray foam Roof deck or walls | Closed-cell spray foam Roof deck or walls
- R-value per inch: Blown-in fiberglass About 2.5 | Open-cell spray foam About 3.5 to 3.9 | Closed-cell spray foam About 6 to 7
R-value measures how well insulation resists heat flow. Higher is better. The bag label on the insulation we install lists the exact R-value per inch.

- Little or no insulation | Target R-49 | Total blown-in depth About 17 inches | Depth to add About 17 inches
- 3 to 4 inches in place | Target R-38 | Total blown-in depth About 13.5 inches | Depth to add About 9 to 11 inches
New homes inside the City of Houston must meet R-49 at the ceiling under the 2021 energy code, in effect since January 1, 2024.

Depths come from the Owens Corning AttiCat coverage chart: R-38 takes 13.5 inches and 19 bags per 1,000 sq ft, and R-49 takes 17 inches and 25 bags.

### My attic insulation is already at R-49. Do I need more?

Not if the depth and condition check out. We measure the depth in several spots on the walkthrough. If your attic is at R-49 and the insulation is dry and even, you do not need more. If it has settled, shifted, or has gaps, we top it up to the target.

## Where Do Houston Homes Leak Air?

Air leaks let cool air out and hot attic air in. Insulation slows heat, but it does not stop air, so we seal first and insulate second. Air sealing and insulation work best together.

### The usual leak spots in a Houston attic are:

- Recessed can lights
- Gaps around wires and plumbing pipes
- The attic hatch or pull down stairs
- Duct boots and bath fan housings
- The tops of interior walls
We seal these with foam or caulk before any blown-in fiberglass goes down. Insulation that sits on top of open gaps loses much of its value.

## Attic Insulation Questions Houston Homeowners Ask

### Is it cheaper to blow in attic insulation myself?

Yes, it costs less upfront because you skip the labor, but you still pay for materials and a machine rental. The savings shrink if you miss air leaks or skip baffles, because insulation over open gaps loses much of its value. A pro covers those steps as part of the plan.

Doing it yourself means finding and sealing leaks first and installing baffles at the eaves so the vents stay clear. Attics can pass 130°F in summer, and the job takes a full day or more. A crew usually finishes faster and does the work in that heat for you.

### Do I need to leave the house during insulation installation?

You can stay home during blown-in fiberglass installation, but leaving during the work is better. For spray foam, leave during the work and stay out until the product's re-entry time passes. We give you that time in writing before we start.

### Can I hire a reliable contractor to inspect my home's attic insulation?

Yes. Our attic walkthrough is free, and it is an inspection. We measure the depth in several spots, and we check for moisture, air leaks, vents, and signs of pests. Then we give you the price in writing. You can take that quote and shop around. There is no fee and no pressure to book the work.

### Do you need a permit to insulate an attic in Houston?

Most attic insulation jobs in Houston need no permit. A permit can come into play when the job involves structural changes or a major renovation. We check your job on the free walkthrough and tell you there if a permit is needed, before any work starts.

### Do you offer a veterans discount?

Yes, we offer a veterans discount. The discount depends on the project, so there is no fixed number. We work it out on the free walkthrough and put it in your written quote. Ask about it when you call or on the form.
